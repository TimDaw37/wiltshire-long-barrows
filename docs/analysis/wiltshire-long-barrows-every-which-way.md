# Long barrows every which way

Tim Daw · [sarsen.org](https://www.sarsen.org/) · September 2026

There is a map now.

**[The Wiltshire Long Barrow Gazetteer](https://timdaw37.github.io/wiltshire-long-barrows/)**

A hundred and twenty-nine Neolithic long barrows on the Wiltshire chalk — HER certain and possible, plus one modern mound at All Cannings that is mine. Gold markers you can trust; grey ones you should treat as possible. Where the earthwork still shows on the LiDAR chip, a rim arrow marks the long **axis** of the mound: the line of the bank, not the way a façade “faces”. **Eighty-six** arrows. Forty-three sites with no arrow, because nothing usable is there to measure.

If you cannot see the mound, you should not invent its bearing.

People have always wanted a sentence for the set. They face the rising sun. They run along the ridges. They stand on the skyline, landscape controllers, meant to be seen from below.

We measured all three.

They do **not** face the sun as a shared design. The eighty-six axes spread. There is a gentle east–west smear and no spike at midsummer or midwinter. Give a random set of arrows the same two solstice targets and chance is as close as the real mounds. They sit nearer the contour than luck would put them. They do not glue themselves to the ridge. Half the sample is still more than thirty degrees off the local ground. West Kennet rides the nose of its spur. East Kennet, a mile away and the same tradition, cuts **across** it.

They do **not** stand on the skyline as a county recipe either. Adam’s Grave does, and Winterbourne Stoke 1, and a handful of others. Kitchen Barrow is a real silhouette from the southern vale — I have the photograph. West Kennet can silhouette from the north; that photograph is real too, and it is a pocket, not the typical approach. South Street and Lanhill, good surviving mounds, fail the test: often visible, almost never the crest against the sky.

So: local care, no single recipe. Field, McOmish, Ruggles and Roberts already said so in other words. The map and the longer notes are that argument with arrows on it.
